Project case study

NATO Strategic Simulator

An independent educational project about institutional decision-making, uncertainty and trade-offs, designed as a strategic learning tool rather than a combat simulator.

People often say, “I would have done X.” The simulator asks what changes when you have to decide before you know how the story ends.

The project places the player in a fictional NATO-related decision environment. There is no single “correct” route: options shift political, institutional and operational pressures and create consequences that may only become visible later.

Research method

For each NATO-related process included in the simulator, I:

  • identify and document the sources used;
  • map the real process before adapting it;
  • record simplifications and areas of uncertainty;
  • flag elements that require practitioner review;
  • prepare focused validation questions where needed.
Publication status: the interactive simulator is published as an independent browser-based educational prototype. It runs directly in the browser on desktop and mobile, with no account or installation required. Launch simulator ↗
Trade-offs, not triviaThe player is asked to balance plausible priorities rather than recall facts.
Layered guidanceBriefings, contextual prompts and analysis introduce information when it becomes useful without removing uncertainty.
Institutionally groundedReal processes inform the scenario, while fictionalisation protects the learning space and keeps it public-safe.
Debrief as learningThe end state explains patterns and branch logic instead of merely displaying a score.

04 · About

I’m Hilal Şahin.

I study International Relations and Political Science at Ankara Medipol University. My interests sit at the intersection of security, geopolitics and technology, particularly how institutions respond when decisions are politically sensitive, information is imperfect and the costs of getting it wrong are unevenly distributed.

I am especially interested in defence and security, AI governance, human rights, organised crime, counter-terrorism, nuclear security and the institutional processes that translate strategy into action.

Alongside research and writing, I build projects that make those systems easier to understand. The NATO Strategic Simulator is the clearest example: rather than telling a player what the “right” decision is, it asks them to experience the trade-offs that make real decisions difficult.
